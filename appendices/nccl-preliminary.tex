\chapter{Exploratory Symmetric-Memory Study}\label{app:nccl-preliminary}

This appendix presents a later exploratory NCCL comparison, separate from the main campaign. Only summary timings are recorded, with insufficient metadata to determine whether GPU initiation was used or whether the improvements carry over to an application. The results suggest follow-up experiments and are excluded from the research-question answers.

\section{Protocol and Measurement Limitations}\label{sec:nccl-preliminary-method}

The study compares NCCL~2.18.5 with ordinary allocation, NCCL~2.31.2 with ordinary allocation, and NCCL~2.31.2 with \texttt{ncclMemAlloc} and symmetric window registration on four A100 GPUs. The summary contains three-run median latencies for 21 allreduce and 19 all-to-all sizes. Comparing versions tests a library update; comparing the two 2.31.2 configurations tests allocation and registration together.

Symmetric buffers can also be used by host-called collectives, so their presence does not show that user kernels invoked NCCL's device API \cite{nvidia_nccl_registration_2026,nvidia_nccl_device_2026}. The record lacks the datatype, exact source and launch configuration, timing endpoint, warm-up, correctness output, and individual trial timings. Without those details, uncertainty intervals cannot be reconstructed and the eight-byte result cannot be assumed to measure one float64 sum.

\subsection{Small Allreduce Improvements}

For message sizes from 4\,B to 32\,KiB, symmetric allocation and registration reduce latency by a factor of approximately 1.54--1.74 relative to 2.18.5. At eight bytes, latency falls from 12.4 to 7.5\,$\mu$s, a reduction of approximately 40\%. Plain 2.31.2 remains at 12.6\,$\mu$s, so the version update alone does not reproduce this small-message gain. The advantage reverses at intermediate sizes, including 256 and 512\,KiB.

The eight-byte result motivates a follow-up using one float64 sum with a known invocation method and timing endpoint. Matching those details would allow a comparison with aCG's scalar-reduction cost.

\subsection{All-to-All Does Not Share the Small-Message Gain}

Small-message all-to-all changes little between the plain and symmetric 2.31.2 configurations, and both are slower than 2.18.5 from 64\,B to 32\,KiB. Larger messages show a modest benefit; at 16\,MiB the symmetric configuration is approximately 1.19 times faster than plain 2.31.2.

Inspecting a source directory does not identify which NCCL kernel ran during a measurement. Host collectives, grouped point-to-point calls, and user device-API kernels invoke different implementations \cite{nvidia_nccl_collectives_2026,nvidia_nccl_registration_2026}. Reports of inter-node proxy regressions and GDAKI stalls also lack timing tables and diagnostic logs, leaving their magnitude and causes unresolved. A useful next experiment is a validated one-double intra-node reduction with its invocation method and completion recorded, followed by a solver test if the improvement persists.
