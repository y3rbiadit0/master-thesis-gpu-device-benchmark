\frontchapter{Abstract}
As AI model training scales across growing numbers of GPUs, communication becomes a bottleneck. Most communication is host-orchestrated: launches and synchronization can leave devices waiting. GPU initiation moves control into kernels and, when supported, posts network work directly to the NIC without CPU involvement. It is not inherently faster; pattern, size, topology, synchronization, and progress determine the benefit. This thesis identifies those conditions across communication models and validates them in a Conjugate Gradient (CG) solver on the Leonardo Booster partition.

We develop \emph{gpu-comm-benchmark}, a CUDA/SYCL suite comparing MPI, NCCL/oneCCL, NVSHMEM, and OSHMPI across point-to-point, halo, collective, and CG-inspired patterns. We also extend oneCCL with grouped NCCL point-to-point, a complete OSHMPI backend, and a validated NVSHMEM runtime and staging foundation, and extend aCG with OSHMPI and SYCL implementations.

Experiments on 1--32 GPUs expose a decision boundary rather than one winner. MPI minimizes startup cost; GPU-initiated NVSHMEM leads large steady multi-node halos; NCCL paths lead large allreduce and multi-node all-to-all. MPI/UCC avoids a 17--34$\times$ small-message all-to-all slowdown at 16 and 32 ranks, while its larger multi-node allreduce configurations benefit above a size-dependent crossover around 64\,KiB. In the reconciled synthetic \texttt{cg\_step} archive, OSHMPI has the lowest point estimate in 25 of 30 multi-rank cells, including every multi-node cell; NCCL leads the remaining five and the single-GPU controls.

Comparing \texttt{cg\_step} with aCG gives 7.8--11.1\% mean absolute error for the matched MPI and NCCL scalar-reduction costs. The regular synthetic halo misses the application's irregular peers and overlap, and the archived OSHMPI paths also differ in scalar primitive and result placement. NCCL is the fastest stable host-initiated solver backend. Device-side NVSHMEM scales better than blocking OSHMPI at larger rank counts, but absent IBGDA leaves inter-node RMA CPU-proxied. The evidence therefore supports configured performance regimes and selective primitive-level prediction, while complete application selection requires matching communication structure and completion dependencies.
