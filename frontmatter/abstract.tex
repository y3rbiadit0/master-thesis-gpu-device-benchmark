\frontchapter{Abstract}
As scientific computing and \ac{AI} workloads scale across \acp{GPU}, intra- and inter-node communication can limit \ac{HPC} application performance. Host-orchestrated launches and synchronization can leave devices waiting while GPU initiation moves communication calls into kernels removing that issue. The issue comes when transports don't allow direct GPU posting of network requests. This thesis evaluates in which cases GPU-initiated could outperform host-driven alternatives and whether benchmark findings transfer to a \ac{CG} solver.

We develop \texttt{gpu-comm-benchmark}, a \ac{CUDA} and SYCL suite comparing \ac{MPI}, \ac{NCCL}, \ac{oneCCL}, NVSHMEM, and \ac{OSHMPI} through transfers, halo exchanges, collectives, and application-inspired patterns. Experiments on Leonardo Booster use up to 32 NVIDIA A100 GPUs across eight nodes, followed by the \ac{aCG} solver on two sparse matrices. Contributions also include grouped NCCL point-to-point support and an OSHMPI backend for oneCCL, a validated NVSHMEM runtime foundation, and OSHMPI and SYCL extensions to aCG.

Scaling up within a node and scaling out across nodes favor different paths. MPI supplies the low-startup reference; persistent, kernel-invoked NVSHMEM leads sustained multi-node halos at approximately 41\,GB/s of aggregate logical bandwidth, against 34--36\,GB/s for MPI; NCCL-based paths lead bulk multi-node collectives. NVSHMEM's intra-node competitiveness does not generally extend across nodes under the evaluated networking, where a \ac{CPU} proxy services device-issued requests. GPU initiation therefore offers workload-specific benefits rather than a universal advantage.

In aCG, matched scalar-reduction costs agree closely with benchmarks, while irregular peers and computation--communication overlap limit halo-level agreement. The SYCL solver takes 0.87--1.02 times the native CUDA time within a node and continues strong-scaling to 32 GPUs with MPI; host-synchronous oneCCL reductions add waiting across nodes. This performance-portability case study demonstrates native-performance retention on the evaluated NVIDIA platform, therefore Backend selection must consider communication structure, topology, and completion dependencies, rather than initiation alone.
