\frontchapter{Abstract}
As AI training scales across more GPUs, communication becomes a bottleneck. Most communication is host-orchestrated, so launches and synchronization can leave devices waiting. GPU invocation moves control into kernels, and some transports also let the GPU post network work directly to the Network Interface Controller (NIC); neither is inherently faster. This thesis evaluates complete communication paths and their behavior in a Conjugate Gradient (CG) solver on Leonardo Booster.

We develop \emph{gpu-comm-benchmark}, a CUDA/SYCL suite comparing MPI, NCCL/oneCCL, NVSHMEM, and OSHMPI across point-to-point, halo, collective, and CG-inspired patterns. We extend oneCCL with grouped NCCL point-to-point, an OSHMPI backend, and an NVSHMEM foundation, and extend aCG with OSHMPI and SYCL implementations.

Experiments on 1--32 GPUs expose a decision boundary rather than one winner. MPI minimizes startup cost; persistent, kernel-invoked NVSHMEM leads large steady multi-node halos; NCCL paths lead large allreduce and multi-node all-to-all. MPI/UCC avoids a 17--34$\times$ small-message all-to-all slowdown at 16 and 32 ranks. In the synthetic \texttt{cg\_step}, OSHMPI has the lowest estimate in 25 of 30 multi-rank cells, but its scalar result stays in host memory; NCCL leads the other five.

In aCG, \texttt{cg\_step} predicts matched MPI and NCCL scalar reductions within 7.8--11.1\%, but not the application's irregular, overlapped halo. NCCL gives the lowest host-initiated solver times; NVSHMEM, with CPU-proxy inter-node networking, improves over OSHMPI at scale but stays behind NCCL. The SYCL solver matches native aCG within a node and keeps scaling to 32 GPUs with MPI; across nodes, its host-synchronous oneCCL reductions remain the gap. Application-level selection must therefore match communication structure and completion dependencies, not initiation alone.
