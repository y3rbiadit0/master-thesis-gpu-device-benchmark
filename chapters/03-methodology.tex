\chapter{Experimental Methodology}\label{ch:m-methodology}

\section{Research Design}

The experimental design follows three levels of increasing application context. Isolated microbenchmarks first characterize primitive and structured communication patterns over message-size and topology sweeps. A composite \texttt{cg\_step} benchmark then executes the communication skeleton of one CG iteration. Finally, the complete aCG application combines communication with sparse matrix--vector multiplication, vector operations, convergence tests, and repeated dependencies. GPU-initiated NVSHMEM neighbor kernels and the monolithic device-side solver provide the initiation evidence for RQ1. For RQ2, direct NVSHMEM and OSHMPI measurements and their application integration expose one-sided performance and programming trade-offs relative to MPI and NCCL. The oneCCL OSHMPI backend, NVSHMEM foundation, and grouped NCCL implementation additionally provide construction and semantic evidence about a common interface; only the complete oneCCL OSHMPI and NCCL paths contribute performance measurements through that interface. RQ3 combines application-transfer evidence from the extended aCG suite with the separate CUDA--SYCL solver-time comparison.

The unit of comparison is a complete runtime stack. ``MPI'' below means the installed GPU-aware HPC-X Open MPI over UCX, and ``oneCCL'' is always qualified by its NCCL or OSHMPI backend. This avoids attributing to an API a result that may have been caused by a transport, collective component, progress engine, or staging policy.

\section{Communication Benchmark Suite}\label{sec:benchmark-method}

The benchmark suite is organized by pattern rather than by library. Every implementation produces a common machine-readable record containing the backend, topology, logical byte count, elapsed time, bandwidth, validation result, and distribution statistics. Backend-specific code is kept below this reporting layer, allowing equivalent operations to be launched through a common experiment matrix.

The ping-pong benchmark reports one directional payload and half the round-trip time. The halo benchmark reports the aggregate logical volume of the two sends and two receives; both isolated exchanges and batches of 100 steady-state exchanges are measured. Allreduce reports the logical input size and ring-equivalent bus bandwidth. All-to-all reports a per-peer size, while its aggregate byte field includes every destination, including the local block. These conventions are not interchangeable, so comparisons are made within a pattern rather than between bandwidth values from different patterns.

The \texttt{cg\_step} benchmark block-distributes a two-dimensional grid by columns. Each iteration packs boundary columns, exchanges the nonperiodic west/east halo, computes a four-neighbor stencil, computes two local dot products, and performs two separate double-precision global sums. It intentionally omits CG vector updates and convergence. Grid sides of 512, 1024, 2048, 4096, and 8192 are swept; the logical halo volume scales with the side and excludes the reductions. With \texttt{GPU\_BENCH\_CG\_PHASES=1}, a second pass measures pack, halo, computation, and reduction separately. The pass synchronizes at each phase boundary and therefore removes overlap available to the unsplit loop. Its sum is diagnostic and is never substituted for the headline \texttt{usec} value.

The MoE benchmark implements top-1 routing with uniform, locality-biased, and hotspot distributions. It is part of the suite's design but is excluded from the quantitative conclusions in this chapter because the inspected tuned result set contains no complete MoE campaign.

\subsection{Topology Matrix}

Topology identifiers use the form \texttt{$N$n$G$g}, where $N$ is the node count and $G$ the GPUs and ranks per node. Ping-pong uses \texttt{1n2g} and \texttt{2n1g}. Halo additionally uses \texttt{1n4g}, \texttt{2n4g}, \texttt{4n4g}, and \texttt{8n4g}. The collective and application-inspired benchmarks also include \texttt{1n1g} as a no-communication control. The largest benchmark cell therefore contains 32 ranks on eight nodes. Single-node cells exercise NVLink, \texttt{2n1g} isolates an inter-node pair, and the remaining cells combine intra- and inter-node traffic.

\subsection{Timing, Validation, and Statistics}

Except for ping-pong, each rank records one elapsed-time series. Sample $i$ is reduced using the slowest rank,
\begin{equation}
t_i = \max_{r \in \{0,\ldots,P-1\}} t_{i,r},
\end{equation}
because a distributed step cannot finish before its slowest participant. The reported latency is the arithmetic mean of this reduced series; quartiles, median, standard deviation, minimum, and maximum describe the same samples. Ping-pong instead reports half of rank zero's round-trip series.

The ping-pong, halo, and allreduce datasets normally contain five independently scheduled jobs per benchmark cell and three trials within each job. Across-job summaries first average the trials from one job and then report the median, quartiles, and sample standard deviation over job means. The analyzed all-to-all and final \texttt{cg\_step} datasets instead contain three jobs with three trials per cell; \texttt{cg\_step} covers 210 topology, grid-size, and backend cells. Every benchmark performs an untimed correctness check and combines the rank-local verdicts globally; every final \texttt{cg\_step} trial passed.

\section{Runtime Configuration and Tuning}\label{sec:runtime-method}

The MPI and OSHMPI configurations use the UCX PML, disable the legacy OpenIB BTL and HCOLL, and enable Open MPI's CUDA support. Single-node jobs select \texttt{sm,cuda,self}; multi-node jobs select \texttt{sm,rc,cuda,self}, service level~1, and two rendezvous rails. GPU Direct RDMA is requested explicitly. The benchmark uses \texttt{get\_zcopy} with a 1024-byte rendezvous threshold. Although each Leonardo node exposes four \texttt{mlx5} devices, a controlled two-versus-four-rail comparison supports the two-rail production setting: four rails harvest otherwise idle adapters for some one-GPU-per-node transfers, but reduce CUDA MPI halo bandwidth by 4--9\% when four GPUs per node already occupy the available HCAs. OSHMPI is unchanged in that saturated regime.

UCC is controlled per operation. The installed library reports version~1.4.0. It is enabled for allreduce and for the tuned MPI all-to-all because it accelerates device collectives over most of their measured ranges and removes a severe small-message all-to-all collapse at 16 and 32 ranks. The policy is not uniformly optimal: on multi-node allreduce, UCC is slower below 64\,KiB and faster at and above that threshold. It is disabled for \texttt{cg\_step}; the paired eight-byte reduction sweep shows gains of 30--50\% within one node but penalties of 25--49\% on the multi-node topologies that determine the selected default. It remains disabled for MoE, whose all-to-all-v path has not been measured. This policy is part of the evaluated stack rather than a post-hoc change to individual points. A short \texttt{UCC\_COLL\_TRACE} run identifies dispatch as \texttt{CL\_BASIC \{TL\_UCP\}} for CUDA all-to-all; because it used one warm-up and two timed iterations, its first-use UCX connection costs make its timings unusable, and only the dispatch record is retained as evidence.

The direct OSHMPI benchmarks additionally select where reduction operands reside. The standalone allreduce defaults to CUDA symmetric operands with UCC enabled, preserving device residency for its full payload sweep. In \texttt{cg\_step}, the reduction-memory switch defaults to \texttt{staged}: fields and halo buffers remain CUDA-resident, and the dot-product kernels produce two device-resident partials, but the reduction phase copies those two doubles to host symmetric memory and calls \texttt{shmem\_double\_sum\_to\_all} on host operands. The synthetic step does not consume the reduced values in a later kernel, so its timed path contains no return copy to the device. The alternative \texttt{device} mode allocates the partials, results, and work arrays from the OSHMPI CUDA symmetric space and reduces them directly, but it requires UCC. Without UCC, OSHMPI reaches a host Open MPI reduction operator that cannot access the device pointers, so the benchmark rejects that combination instead of allowing it to fail. The staged and device paths are therefore reported as distinct configured variants rather than silently pooled.

NCCL retains its default algorithm and protocol selection; multi-node runs set service level~1. The oneCCL/NCCL configuration uses one worker, automatic worker affinity, and NCCL for payload communication. NVSHMEM uses MPI bootstrap, the \texttt{ibrc} remote transport, a 1\,GiB symmetric heap, NCCL collective dispatch, and no IBGDA. Its persistent inter-node ping-pong and halo kernels cap the cooperative grid at eight blocks to avoid overwhelming the host proxy. Direct OSHMPI uses a 1\,GiB symmetric heap, GPU features enabled, and the UCX OSC component. The oneCCL/OSHMPI configuration uses a 2\,GiB heap, a 256\,MiB staging arena, and an 8\,MiB point-to-point slot per peer.

The word \emph{tuned} must be interpreted cautiously. The current harness encodes these policies, but the aggregated JSON artifacts do not preserve a complete environment snapshot, source revision, node list, or placement record. They also store the selected final configuration rather than UCC on/off as a case axis. Consequently, the UCC effect sizes rely on the separately documented paired campaign and cannot be reconstructed from the final aggregate alone; no tuned-versus-untuned difference is inferred from backend curves collected under unknown settings.

\section{oneCCL Extensions}

\subsection{Grouped NCCL Point-to-Point Operations}

The oneCCL NCCL backend is extended so that \nolinkurl{ccl::group_start} and \nolinkurl{ccl::group_end} dispatch to \nolinkurl{ncclGroupStart} and \nolinkurl{ncclGroupEnd}. The implementation handles nested groups, propagates backend errors, and returns events associated with the deferred NCCL work. This is required for mutually dependent NCCL send/receive batches and is used by the halo and all-to-all implementations.

\subsection{OSHMPI Backend}

The added OSHMPI backend is selected with \texttt{CCL\_BACKEND=oshmpi}. oneCCL collectives map to OpenSHMEM team operations where an equivalent exists: barrier to team synchronization, allreduce to typed reductions, broadcast to \texttt{shmem\_broadcastmem}, allgather to \texttt{shmem\_fcollectmem}, and all-to-all to \texttt{shmem\_alltoallmem}. Operations without a direct equivalent require composition: all-to-all-v uses one-sided puts plus synchronization, rooted reduce discards the non-root outputs of an allreduce, and reduce-scatter combines an allreduce with a local slice.

The backend initializes OSHMPI lazily and verifies that the requested rank mapping matches \texttt{SHMEM\_TEAM\_WORLD}. It uses a no-op key-value store because process management already establishes the PEs. Ordinary oneCCL buffers are staged through a grow-only symmetric arena by default; a direct path is implementation-specific and therefore not used as the portable semantic baseline. OSHMPI operations are host-driven and blocking. Pending SYCL dependencies must complete before entry, and the returned oneCCL event is already complete. Group calls preserve correctness but cannot fuse work as the NCCL backend does.

\subsection{NVSHMEM Backend Foundation}

The separate NVSHMEM prototype is selected with \texttt{CCL\_BACKEND=nvshmem} when oneCCL is built with optional NVSHMEM support. It accepts only CUDA-backed SYCL device communicators; host communicators continue to use the native backend. The implementation validates device and context membership, reuses oneCCL's key-value store to distribute an NVSHMEM unique identifier, checks PE/rank agreement, tracks whether oneCCL owns NVSHMEM initialization, and creates one process-global fixed symmetric arena. Device and shared USM buffers can be copied through deterministic chunks in that arena without requiring application-side NVSHMEM allocation.

The implementation was validated in three bounded stages on Leonardo. A mixed DPC++/NVCC spike executed an NVSHMEM on-stream reduction on a native CUDA stream extracted from a SYCL queue and observed completion through a SYCL event on one and two nodes. Backend-selection tests then verified optional builds, CUDA communicator construction, native host-communicator behavior, and explicit rejection when NVSHMEM support is absent. Finally, one- and two-node runtime tests covered UID bootstrap, communicator reuse, shared/device USM staging below and above arena-lane boundaries, expected host-pointer rejection, and ownership-safe finalization.

This status is narrower than the OSHMPI backend. Public oneCCL barrier, collective, point-to-point, and split operations still report unsupported-operation errors; collective dependency handling and returned-event semantics remain unimplemented. No oneCCL/NVSHMEM performance result appears in this thesis. The standalone validated collective spike is host-enqueued through \texttt{nvshmemx\_*\_on\_stream}, not kernel-initiated. Together, the spike and runtime foundation establish compiler, bootstrap, memory, and lifecycle prerequisites for a later collective implementation and a separate device-initiated prototype.

\section{Application-Level Validation with aCG}\label{sec:acg-method}

aCG is a suite of GPU-accelerated iterative solvers based on Conjugate Gradient and pipelined CG \cite{trotter_cpu-_2025}. This work extends its CUDA host-initiated solver with an OSHMPI communicator. The controlled campaign compares host-initiated MPI, NCCL, NVSHMEM, and OSHMPI versions of the same solver, and includes the monolithic device-side NVSHMEM solver as a separate execution-model experiment. The result tree contains 507 completed logs: 354 strict four-communicator trials, 90 device-NVSHMEM trials, and 63 single-GPU controls. The device variant is informative about GPU initiation but is not a strict communicator substitution.

The sparse matrix is partitioned once with METIS and the same stored partition is reused by every communicator. This prevents partition variation from changing halo volume or load balance. Two SuiteSparse matrices are used: Bump\_2911 with 2,911,419 rows and 127,729,899 full symmetric nonzeros, and Queen\_4147 with 4,147,110 rows and 329,499,284 full symmetric nonzeros. Runs use 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, and 32 processes, corresponding to one GPU per process through eight four-GPU nodes.

Every trial uses a manufactured solution with seed~101, relative residual tolerance $10^{-6}$, zero absolute tolerance, and a maximum of 100,000 iterations. Three trials are launched per job. Following the reference aCG evaluation, the primary report selects the fastest converged trial and computes FLOP/s per GPU from the matrix dimensions, nonzeros, and executed iteration count. Throughput is preferred to solver time when comparing communicators because floating-point reduction order can change the number of iterations. A second, median-based analysis is retained as a sensitivity check rather than mixed into the primary ranking. For MPI, whose regimes can change within one allocation, job-level minimum, median, and maximum are reported instead of treating a pooled median as a representative execution.

\subsection{OSHMPI Communication Path}

The OSHMPI port allocates CUDA-resident symmetric halo buffers through the OSHMPI space extension. Remote buffer offsets are exchanged once through MPI. Each halo exchange then synchronizes the CUDA stream, enters a global barrier, issues one blocking put per recipient, calls \texttt{shmem\_quiet}, enters a second barrier, and unpacks the received data. Scalar reductions synchronize the stream and use CUDA-aware \texttt{MPI\_Allreduce}; they do not use OpenSHMEM reductions.

This path is deliberately simple but has an important consequence: the complete transfer finishes in the halo-begin function before the local sparse matrix--vector product starts, so OSHMPI cannot overlap halo communication with local computation. By contrast, host NVSHMEM enqueues put-with-signal and reduction operations on the stream, while the monolithic NVSHMEM variant issues communication from GPU code. OSHMPI is not available to the device-side solver because the required CUDA atomics and signal implementation are not functional in the evaluated upstream version.

The aCG campaign shares the benchmark's HPC-X and UCX policy but retains two historical differences for comparability with already collected results: the UCX rendezvous threshold is 16,384 bytes, and \texttt{NVSHMEM\_DISABLE\_NCCL=1}. The latter forces host NVSHMEM reductions away from the NCCL-dispatched path used in the microbenchmark and is treated as a configuration limitation, not as an inherent property of NVSHMEM. If the solver performs $K$ iterations, its counters contain $K+1$ halo exchanges and $2K+2$ allreduces because setup and finalization add one operation of each kind. The steady iteration nevertheless has exactly the same high-level skeleton as \texttt{cg\_step}: one halo exchange followed by two eight-byte global sums.

\subsection{Application Metrics and Correctness}

The primary metrics are solver time and GFLOP/s per GPU. CUDA-event profiling additionally reports sparse matrix--vector multiplication, dot product, vector update, residual norm, copy, halo, allreduce, and residual ``other'' time. These labels are not all direct operation costs. The halo column brackets only the wait remaining after potential overlap; OSHMPI completes communication before that interval, so most of its communication appears in ``other''. NVSHMEM can fuse communication into the matrix--vector phase. Allreduce includes both collective execution and rank-arrival skew.

Correctness requires successful termination and a relative residual below $10^{-6}$. Iteration counts, residuals, and manufactured-solution error are retained for every communicator. They are used to distinguish numerical effects of reduction order from communication corruption.

\subsection{CUDA--SYCL Performance Retention}

The SYCL implementation developed in this work addresses the performance-retention part of RQ3 through a separate implementation comparison. MPI provides its distributed runtime and halo exchange, while a solver option changes only the scalar reductions between MPI and oneCCL. Its available report uses median solver time and covers Bump\_2911 through 16 GPUs, so those values are reported separately and are not pooled with the fastest-trial CUDA campaign. Performance retention is assessed through the reported SYCL-to-CUDA MPI solver-time ratios at each GPU count: a ratio of one indicates equal time, and a ratio above one indicates additional time in the SYCL implementation.

The comparison also serves as a portable-compute control for interpreting communication rankings. The composite benchmark's communication-free cases provide supporting evidence about kernel and submission costs, but do not isolate the causes of the complete solver's performance gap. The available configurations and distinct reporting policies constrain attribution and statistical comparison. The resulting evidence concerns performance retained by this SYCL implementation on NVIDIA A100 GPUs, rather than performance portability across accelerator vendors.

\section{Preliminary NCCL Version and Memory-Path Comparison}\label{sec:nccl-preliminary-method}

A separate exploratory \texttt{nccl-tests} study compares three configurations on one Leonardo Booster node with four A100 GPUs: NCCL~2.18.5 from NVHPC~24.5 with \texttt{cudaMalloc}; NCCL~2.31.2 with \texttt{cudaMalloc}; and NCCL~2.31.2 with \texttt{ncclMemAlloc} and symmetric window registration. The available author-supplied note, \emph{GDAKI) Results}, reports median latency over three runs. All 21 allreduce and 19 all-to-all size rows are transcribed in \path{data/nccl-preliminary.csv}, with provenance in the adjacent \path{nccl-preliminary.md}. These are summary data from a later experiment, not additional samples of the main seven-stack campaign.

The three-way comparison distinguishes an observed version change (2.18.5 plain versus 2.31.2 plain) from a combined allocation/registration change within 2.31.2. It does not isolate allocation, registration, or algorithm selection individually. The note labels the last configuration ``LSA'', but symmetric registration can be used by host-called collectives as well as by user device-API kernels \cite{nvidia_nccl_registration_2026,nvidia_nccl_device_2026}. Without the tested source revision, launch commands, implementation selector, and kernel trace, the invocation mode is unresolved; this thesis therefore calls it the \emph{symmetric configuration}.

The summary does not record datatype, reduction operator, process/thread layout, in-place mode, timing/completion boundary, warm-up counts, correctness output, or inclusion of setup costs. It reports 1.0--1.1-fold run spread except for a 1.5-fold anomaly at 4\,B in plain NCCL~2.31 allreduce, attributed in the note to warm-up. Individual trials and the spread definition are unavailable, so no confidence intervals or error bars are reconstructed. The 8\,B allreduce cell is not assumed to represent one FP64 scalar. Size labels are retained exactly: the all-to-all bandwidth at 16\,MiB is consistent with total per-rank buffer size and a $(P-1)/P$ bus factor at $P=4$, but this convention needs confirmation from the original records before comparison with the main per-peer sweep. Reported speedups below are recomputed as reference latency divided by symmetric latency using the rounded medians.

\section{Reproducibility Policy}

The cluster directories in both repositories are the executable specification of the experiments. They record module stacks, build presets, launcher choice, topology matrices, process-to-GPU mapping, transport variables, solver flags, and result-reduction scripts. OSHMPI and oneCCL must use the same MPI installation, and the aCG campaign prints the selected transport configuration. Remaining provenance gaps in the archived aggregates are reported explicitly rather than reconstructed from assumptions.

For the preliminary NCCL study, the archived transcription and plotting script reproduce the presentation only. They do not establish the missing runtime metadata or replace the raw trial logs. The figures are generated from the CSV by \path{tools/plot_nccl_preliminary.py} at the repository root.
