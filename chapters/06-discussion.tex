\chapter{Discussion and Validity}\label{ch:m-discussion}

\section{Answer to RQ1: Conditions for Beneficial GPU Initiation}

The main campaign answers RQ1 with performance regions, not a universal winner. HPC-X MPI provides the lowest startup latency for point-to-point and isolated neighbor operations and remains strongest throughout the inter-node ping-pong sweep. The persistent NVSHMEM implementation therefore does not eliminate MPI's startup advantage. Its favorable region is large steady-state halo exchange: one cooperative kernel contains repeated communication, amortizing host launch cost across the batch while retaining per-iteration device signaling. This is evidence for that configured path, not an isolated estimate of the benefit of initiation alone.

Host-initiated alternatives remain competitive for other patterns. NCCL-based stacks lead large allreduce and multi-node all-to-all; tuned MPI/UCC leads small-message all-to-all at high rank counts, while UCC changes from a penalty to a benefit around 64\,KiB in the larger multi-node allreduce configurations. Host-staged OSHMPI has the lowest estimate in 25 of 30 multi-rank \texttt{cg\_step} cells, including all multi-node cells. Its archived result remains in host memory, so this ranking does not establish the cost of a solver-ready reduction. The auxiliary device/UCC control raises complete-step time by 47--70\%, but the concurrent compute increase prevents attribution to reductions alone.

The monolithic NVSHMEM solver supplies complementary evidence: its larger-scale trend improves relative to blocking OSHMPI, consistently with reduced host synchronization, but solver organization changes as well. Leonardo's CPU-proxied inter-node transport prevents these results from establishing the separate benefit of GPU-posted network communication. The later NCCL symmetric-memory study reinforces the importance of operation and size, but its unresolved invocation mode precludes using it as additional evidence for device initiation. The answer to RQ1 is thus conditional on the complete implementation and its completion boundary.

\section{Answer to RQ2: Practical Trade-offs of SHMEM-Style Communication}

\paragraph{Direct adoption: benefits and responsibilities.}
The measured benefit of SHMEM depends on how the exchange is organized. Persistent NVSHMEM leads large steady-state multi-node halos, while direct OSHMPI with host-staged scalar reductions leads the multi-node synthetic \texttt{cg\_step}. A useful one-sided path therefore need not be GPU-initiated. These advantages coexist with MPI's lower startup latency and NCCL's strength in large collectives. In complete aCG, OSHMPI's blocking halo loses the overlap available to other implementations, and NCCL is the fastest stable host-initiated communicator. The different scalar primitives and completion boundaries also limit direct transfer of the synthetic OSHMPI result.

At source level, puts remove the need to post a matching receive for every payload. That can simplify an exchange whose remote destinations and buffer layout are already known. The responsibility shifts to establishing symmetric storage and remote offsets, signaling readiness, and ensuring completion before buffers are consumed or reused. The aCG OSHMPI communicator makes that trade-off explicit: offsets are exchanged during setup, while each halo uses stream synchronization, puts, quiet, and barriers. These are concrete programming consequences rather than measured developer-productivity gains.

\paragraph{NVSHMEM and OSHMPI in practice.}
Both paths require symmetric communication storage, but they provide different ways to integrate it with GPU execution. NVSHMEM supports host-enqueued, stream-ordered work and calls from device code, with signals and waits that can be embedded in persistent kernels. On Leonardo, its inter-node device requests still depend on the \texttt{ibrc} CPU proxy. OSHMPI instead implements OpenSHMEM over MPI, and the evaluated path is host-driven with explicit blocking completion; its aCG halo uses CUDA-resident symmetric allocations, while its scalar solver reductions retain CUDA-aware MPI. Adopting OSHMPI can reuse the installed MPI substrate, whereas NVSHMEM provides tighter CUDA execution integration. Their observed differences include progress, synchronization, memory handling, and operation mapping as well as invocation mode.

\paragraph{Integration beneath a common interface.}

The oneCCL contributions establish what is required to expose communication paths absent from the baseline. The NCCL extension propagates group start and end through the backend, tracks deferred work, handles nesting and failures, and makes mutually dependent send/receive batches usable by halo and all-to-all operations. The complete OSHMPI backend adds runtime selection, communicator and rank validation, datatype and reduction mapping, symmetric staging for ordinary host and SYCL USM buffers, collective implementations, and put-based point-to-point operations with explicit completion. Both preserve the oneCCL application interface, but they cannot erase backend semantics: NCCL remains stream-ordered and asynchronous, whereas the OSHMPI mapping waits for queue dependencies and returns an already-complete event after a blocking host operation.

The NVSHMEM branch exposes a different readiness boundary. It proves optional mixed DPC++/NVCC integration, backend selection, CUDA communicator construction, KVS-based UID bootstrap, ownership-safe lifecycle, and deterministic symmetric staging on one and two nodes. It does not yet implement oneCCL collectives, dependencies, or returned events, and its validated interoperability operation is host-enqueued on a native CUDA stream. It is therefore evidence about the prerequisites and engineering cost of adding NVSHMEM beneath oneCCL, not evidence about collective performance or GPU initiation through the oneCCL API. Reaching that goal requires completion of the asynchronous event contract, collective mappings, and a later kernel-initiated path.

The common engineering requirement is to preserve memory, ordering, and completion contracts across unlike runtimes. Symmetric staging lets ordinary oneCCL buffers enter an OpenSHMEM operation, but adds data movement; returning a completed event preserves completion correctness without preserving asynchronous overlap. The NVSHMEM foundation must solve a different event and stream-ordering problem before it can provide public collectives. Together, the direct measurements and integration results answer RQ2: SHMEM can fit persistent or explicitly managed exchanges well, but its allocation and synchronization responsibilities remain visible either in the application or in an adapter that may add staging and waiting. The benchmark quantifies complete-path costs, while programmability, source-level portability, and readiness are assessed from the implementations rather than from a developer-productivity study.

\section{Answer to RQ3: Application Transfer and CUDA--SYCL Performance Retention}

\paragraph{Matched reduction costs.}
The OSHMPI communicator and SYCL implementation make it possible to test transfer at both the communication-backend and portable-compute levels. The regenerated two-reduction predictions have mean absolute errors of 7.8--11.1\% for MPI and NCCL across the two matrices. Individual cells differ by more than this average, so the result establishes approximate phase-cost estimation rather than a uniform error bound. NVSHMEM additionally requires matching its NCCL-dispatch setting; the separate auxiliary analysis is not interchangeable with the default archived JSON. The regular-halo-to-composite discrepancy also depends on the latter's serialized exchange structure.

The percentage agreement is a reduction-cost comparison, not a prediction of each library's total solver time or scaling. Moreover, aCG's measured OSHMPI communicator calls CUDA-aware \texttt{MPI\_Allreduce}; direct OSHMPI \texttt{cg\_step} uses an OpenSHMEM reduction. Matching those rows by backend name would compare different primitives.

\paragraph{Halo representation and whole-solver ranking.}
The multi-node ranking is not predicted: \texttt{cg\_step} selects OSHMPI, whereas complete aCG selects NCCL as the fastest stable host-initiated communicator. The reconstructed sparse graphs have rank-dependent exchanges, with as many as 17 outgoing neighbors on an individual Bump rank, memory-bound local work, repeated dependencies, and an overlap opportunity that the blocking OSHMPI path cannot use. The measured UCX-threshold sensitivity is too small to explain the large MPI variability, while the NVSHMEM collective policy must be matched explicitly. MPI's slow halo runs also inflate the following collective interval. Reduction order additionally changes convergence, and the archived OSHMPI scalar comparison changes primitive and result placement.

Microbenchmarks should therefore be used as the first stage of selection. A useful prediction must match not only operation and size but also completion boundary, progress model, synchronization, and available overlap. A composite kernel improves the prediction only if it preserves those semantics.

\paragraph{Monolithic GPU-initiated execution.}
The monolithic device-side NVSHMEM solver scales better than blocking OSHMPI at larger process counts, which is consistent with removing repeated host synchronization and fusing communication into device execution. It is not a strict host-versus-device backend substitution because solver organization changes at the same time, and the inter-node path still depends on a CPU proxy. The result therefore supports GPU initiation as an application-level mechanism worth exploiting, but does not establish an isolated or universal speedup from initiation alone.

\paragraph{CUDA--SYCL performance retention.}
The SYCL aCG report gives solver-time ratios of 1.17--1.45 relative to the CUDA reference across the reported one-to-16-GPU Bump\_2911 cells, or 17--45\% additional time. Both sides use medians. The narrowing at 16 GPUs is compatible with changing compute and communication contributions, but the summaries do not isolate its cause. The report's CUDA convergence classification also requires reprocessing: missing parser fields were displayed as a zero converged count. These values therefore remain supporting implementation timings rather than a newly validated equal-accuracy comparison.

The communication-free \texttt{cg\_step} controls provide supporting evidence that kernel and host-submission costs remain visible when communication is absent. They help explain why closely matched CUDA and SYCL MPI microbenchmarks need not imply equal solver performance, but they do not isolate the causes of the aCG gap. Compiler choices, kernels, memory handling, and orchestration remain possible contributors. The answer to this part of RQ3 is therefore a platform-specific measure of performance retention with partial explanatory evidence: the SYCL port incurs a measurable time overhead in the reported cases, while its portable source interface alone does not establish performance parity or cross-vendor efficiency.

\paragraph{Unvalidated candidate from the newer NCCL study.}
The preliminary 8\,B allreduce improvement identifies a candidate, not a matched aCG reduction measurement. Datatype, invocation mode, completion, and runtime settings must first be established. Applying it to \texttt{cg\_step} and aCG is a prospective test of RQ3, separate from the regenerated MPI/NCCL prediction errors.

\section{Threats to Validity}

\paragraph{Internal validity.}
The benchmark implementations use equivalent logical operations but not identical algorithms: NCCL all-to-all is grouped point-to-point, MPI uses a collective, and SHMEM may use puts. The persistent NVSHMEM neighbor kernels also differ from their host-initiated baselines in API, batching, signaling, completion, and sampling granularity, so their stack-level differences cannot be attributed to initiation alone. This is appropriate for stack selection but prevents attribution to the programming model alone. The tuned aggregates do not preserve complete environment manifests, the all-to-all tree merges campaigns collected under different defaults, and oneCCL launch placement is less explicit than the \texttt{srun} mapping. UCC's dispatch is observed in the installed 1.4.0 runtime, while internal algorithm names are checked against the public \texttt{v1.4.x} source branch because no exact public \texttt{v1.4.0} tag is available. The OSHMPI device-path A/B changes operand residency, symmetric allocator, and UCC together, so only its phase-specific observations, not a single-cause interpretation of the total, are supported. MPI regime changes in aCG further weaken large-scale MPI comparisons.

\paragraph{Construct validity.}
Latency and logical bandwidth do not capture every application cost. Steady halo amortizes synchronization, while phase instrumentation deliberately introduces extra synchronization; its sum is diagnostic rather than an alternative headline time. The winning OSHMPI \texttt{cg\_step} result stages two scalar operands through host memory and is therefore a configured-stack result rather than a like-for-like device-resident reduction comparison. The aCG ``halo'' timer measures only exposed wait and under-reports OSHMPI's blocking begin-phase cost. Programmability is assessed through required allocations, synchronization, source integration, and semantic constraints; it is not a controlled developer study or a quantitative software-engineering metric.

\paragraph{External validity.}
All new measurements use Leonardo's A100 Booster partition. Results do not establish performance portability across GPU generations, accelerator vendors, or other network and software configurations. The recorded driver diagnostics and selected \texttt{ibrc} transport support the classification of the evaluated NVSHMEM path as CPU-proxied; the reported lack of IBGDA support is also documented by Trotter et al.\ \cite{trotter_cpu-_2025}. The conclusions therefore do not cover GPU-posted NVSHMEM RMA. Only two sparse matrices validate the complete solver, and all-to-all has no application-level validation in this thesis.

\paragraph{Conclusion validity.}
Ping-pong, halo, and allreduce cells normally contain five jobs and fifteen trial records, while the analyzed all-to-all and final \texttt{cg\_step} cells contain three jobs and nine records. No hypothesis tests or confidence intervals are used, so small differences are not treated as definitive. The aCG primary report follows the reference paper's fastest-converged-trial convention; this facilitates comparison but can favor rare good modes. Median-based rankings are therefore reported only as sensitivity evidence, and MPI is shown by per-job minimum, median, and maximum. Host NVSHMEM in aCG disables NCCL collective dispatch, so its difference from OSHMPI cannot be generalized to a fully tuned NVSHMEM configuration.

\paragraph{Preliminary NCCL evidence.}
The newer NCCL study has only rounded three-run medians and qualitative spread information. Missing source revisions, run commands, datatype, correctness logs, and timing boundaries prevent replication from the summary alone. Comparing 2.18.5 plain with 2.31.2 symmetric changes both version and memory handling; comparing the two 2.31.2 columns still combines allocation and registration. Neither comparison isolates device initiation, and the proposed warm-up and GDAKI failure explanations remain unverified. The study identifies a follow-up candidate after the main scalar-completion and halo-representation gaps are addressed.

\begin{keypoint}[Scope of the claims]
The results establish which complete configured stacks performed best for the measured communication signatures on Leonardo. They do not establish an intrinsic ordering among the MPI, collective, and PGAS programming models.
\end{keypoint}
